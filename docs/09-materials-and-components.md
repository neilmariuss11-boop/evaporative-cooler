# Materials, parts and components

Concept Option B - Scale. Every part of the cooler with its material or specification, why it was chosen, what was rejected, quantity and estimated cost. Item numbers (#) refer to the bill of materials in `05-systems-and-protocol.md` section 7; positions and sizes are in `04-assembly-spec.md`.

Costs are 2026 Metro Manila estimates, ±30 %. Total: about **PHP 24,500**.

## 1. Material rules for the whole unit

The cooler runs at 90-99 % humidity inside, with water running over the pad all day. Most material choices follow from five rules:

1. **Nothing that rusts in the wet zone.** The rear pad module, sump and chamber use plastics, anodised aluminium and stainless steel only. Plain or zinc-plated steel is allowed only in the dry electrical bay and the base frame.
2. **No mixed metals in wet contact.** Steel touching aluminium in water corrodes the aluminium (galvanic corrosion). Aluminium parts are joined with aluminium rivets or stainless fasteners with nylon washers.
3. **No chlorine in the water.** Bleach shortens cellulose pad life and pits aluminium. Algae are controlled by a dark sump, daily pad drying and weekly draining (`05-systems-and-protocol.md` section 2).
4. **The vapour barrier goes on the inside.** On a dry-season afternoon the chamber air holds more water vapour than the outside air (about 3.4 kPa against 2.9 kPa), so vapour pushes outward through the walls. The PVC liner on the inside face, with sealed joints, stops it from soaking the foam and plywood.
5. **Electronics are protected from humidity.** Boards live in the dry bay, are conformal-coated, and the sensors inside the chamber are sealed probes with breathable membrane filters.
